# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We want everyone to be able to take part in Bach, whatever their age, body,
disability, ethnicity, gender identity and expression, level of experience,
nationality, appearance, race, religion or sexual identity and orientation.
We pledge to act and interact in ways that make this a welcoming, open and
healthy community.

## Our standards

Things that help the community:

- Being kind and patient, especially with people who are new.
- Respecting different opinions, viewpoints and experiences.
- Giving feedback that is specific and useful, and taking it gracefully.
- Owning our mistakes, apologising, and learning from them.
- Putting what is best for the whole community ahead of winning an argument.

Things that are not acceptable:

- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Trolling, insults, derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks.
- Harassment, in public or in private.
- Publishing someone else's private information, such as an address or
  email, without their permission.
- Anything else that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting.

## Scope

This applies in every space the project runs: issues, pull requests,
discussions, reviews and commit messages, and when someone represents Bach
in public.

## Enforcement

The maintainers are responsible for explaining and enforcing these
standards, and will take fair action in response to behaviour they find
inappropriate, threatening, offensive or harmful. That can include editing
or removing comments, commits, issues and other contributions, and warning,
temporarily limiting or permanently banning the person responsible. We'll
say why when we do.

To report a problem, contact the maintainers privately by opening a
[private security advisory](https://github.com/usebach/bach/security/advisories/new)
and marking it as a conduct report, or by reaching out to the owners of the
[usebach](https://github.com/usebach) organisation on GitHub. Don't report
it in a public thread. Every report is reviewed and handled in confidence,
and the privacy and safety of the person who reported comes first.

If the report is about a maintainer, say so, and it will be handled by the
others.

## Enforcement guidelines

Maintainers follow these steps, roughly in order of seriousness:

1. **Correction.** A private note explaining what was out of line and why,
   and sometimes a public apology.
2. **Warning.** A warning with consequences for continued behaviour, such
   as no interaction with the people involved for a set time.
3. **Temporary ban.** A time-limited ban from any interaction with the
   project.
4. **Permanent ban.** A permanent ban for a pattern of violations or for
   serious harassment.
